## Anatomy

```tsx
import { musicalGrid } from '@addstack/daw-ui';
import { Timeline } from '@addstack/daw-ui/react';

const grid = musicalGrid({ bpm: 120 });

<Timeline.Root start={0} end={30} read={() => transport.time}>
  <Timeline.Ruler grid={grid}>
    <Timeline.Grid grid={grid} />
  </Timeline.Ruler>
  <div className="relative">
    <Timeline.Grid grid={grid} />
    <Timeline.Track aria-label="Drums">
      <Timeline.Region at={4} duration={8} offset={1.5}>
        <Timeline.RegionHeader>
          <Timeline.RegionLabel>{clip.name}</Timeline.RegionLabel>
        </Timeline.RegionHeader>
        <Timeline.RegionContent>
          <Waveform.Root peaks={peaks} />
        </Timeline.RegionContent>
      </Timeline.Region>
    </Timeline.Track>
  </div>
  <Timeline.Playhead />
</Timeline.Root>
```

`Timeline.Root` is the time axis: `start` … `end` seconds fill its width. A `Track` is a row, one track of an arrangement. A `Region` is what lies on it for a time: a region, clip or pattern (the same thing in Logic, Ableton and FL Studio), a loop range, or a marker with no duration. Its content, a [waveform](/docs/components/waveform), a label, is yours to put inside.

Everything on the axis places itself in CSS from two variables the root writes:

| CSS variable | |
| --- | --- |
| `--timeline-start` | Seconds at the left edge. |
| `--timeline-scale` | CSS pixels per second. |

Scrolling or zooming writes these two, whatever the number of tracks and regions: nothing renders, and nothing runs for each region. The playhead is written, as a number, only into the parts that follow it (`Playhead`, `Waveform.Progress`), so a frame of playback recalculates the style of those parts alone. To follow the playhead with an element of your own, render it as the `Playhead` (`render={<YourLine />}`). Place regions in a `Track`, or another element that spans the timeline's width.

## Regions

A region starts `at` seconds on the timeline, lasts `duration`, and shows its content from `offset` seconds into it, like a clip whose start was trimmed. The content follows the region: a waveform in it shows the audio from `offset` for `duration`.

```tsx
<Timeline.Region at={clip.start} duration={clip.length} offset={clip.offset}>
  <Waveform.Root peaks={clip.peaks} className="h-full" />
</Timeline.Region>
```

A new placement, from props or from `read`, rewrites the region's position and width without rendering what is inside, and a waveform in it draws nothing it has drawn already: moving or trimming a region only places its tiles again. For a placement that changes on its own, pass `read`, called once per animation frame; a take being recorded grows like this:

```tsx
<Timeline.Region at={recordStart} read={() => ({ duration: take.duration })}>
  <Waveform.Root peaks={take} className="h-full" />
</Timeline.Region>
```

Time runs left to right in every language, as in music notation and every DAW, also in a right-to-left page.

## Playback, scrolling and zoom

Pass values that change on every frame as functions, which the timeline calls once per animation frame:

```tsx
<Timeline.Root
  start={0}
  end={30}
  read={() => transport.time}
  readView={() => [view.start, view.end]}
/>
```

`start`, `end` and `position` are for values that change rarely, from React state. The view in the demo zooms with a fader whose `onValueChange` writes to a ref that `readView` reads: dragging it renders nothing.

The timeline does not scroll or zoom by itself: it shows the view you give it, so the gestures and their policy (scroll with the wheel, zoom around the pointer, follow the playhead) stay in your application.

## Editing regions

<ComponentPreview name="timeline-editing" />

With `onRegionsChange`, regions with a `value` are editable: a press selects a region, a drag moves it, its handles trim it. What the user does to one selected region they do to all of them, so the timeline, not each region, runs the gesture and reports it once, as a list of changes:

```tsx
<Timeline.Root
  snap={grid}
  onRegionsChange={(changes, { edit }) => preview(changes)}   // as the gesture goes
  onGestureEnd={(changes) => commit(changes)}                  // [{ value, at, duration, offset, track }]: one undo step
>
  <Timeline.Track value="drums" aria-label="Drums">
    <Timeline.Region value={clip.id} at={clip.start} duration={clip.length} offset={clip.offset} length={clip.fileLength}>
      <Timeline.RegionHeader>
        <ClipMenu clip={clip} />
        <Timeline.RegionLabel>{clip.name}</Timeline.RegionLabel>
      </Timeline.RegionHeader>
      <Timeline.RegionContent>
        <Waveform.Root peaks={clip.peaks} className="h-full" />
      </Timeline.RegionContent>
      <Timeline.RegionHandle side="start" aria-label="Start" />
      <Timeline.RegionHandle side="end" aria-label="End" />
    </Timeline.Region>
  </Timeline.Track>
</Timeline.Root>
```

- **Regions show where a gesture takes them, without rendering.** Their content follows: a waveform places its tiles again, and draws nothing it has drawn. When the gesture ends they return to their props, so keep the changes by updating them, in `onGestureEnd` or as they come.
- **Moving** a region moves the selection with it, along the timeline and to the track under the pointer (`track` is that track's `value`). A press on a control inside a region, such as its menu, does not move it.
- **Trimming** with a handle moves the end, or the start and the content with it, so the audio stays where it was. `length`, the seconds of content, stops a trim where the content ends; without it, as for a pattern that repeats, the end goes on. What the new edge means (a trim, a loop, a stretch) is the application's to decide; the modifier keys are in `details.event`.
- **Snapping** follows the region the user holds, to the finest lines of `snap` at least 12 px apart, those a `Timeline.Grid` shows by default; the others keep their places relative to it. Shift does not snap.
- **Limits hold for the group:** the region that can go least stops them all, at the start of the timeline, at the start or end of its content, and at one snap step long.
- **Selection:** a press selects a region alone, Cmd or Ctrl adds it or takes it out, a press on a track outside its regions clears it. `selected` and `onSelectedChange` control it; regions show it as `data-selected`.

Handles are there only while their region is pointed at, focused, selected or being edited, so regions nobody touches cost nothing. Style them as you like: a visible bar, or a transparent zone that only changes the cursor, as in the demo.

| Input | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Drag a region | Moves the selection; across tracks too. |
| Drag a handle | Trims the selection's start or end. |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> while dragging | Does not snap. |
| Press, <kbd>Cmd</kbd>/<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+press, press on a track | Selects alone, adds or takes out, clears. |
| <kbd>Tab</kbd> | Into the regions, one tab stop for them all, then the focused region's handles. |
| Arrows | The previous or next region on the track, or the nearest on the track above or below. |
| <kbd>Space</kbd> | Selects (with <kbd>Cmd</kbd>/<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>: adds or takes out). |
| <kbd>Cmd</kbd>/<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+arrows | Moves the selection by a snap step (<kbd>Shift</kbd>: a pixel), or to the next track. |
| Arrows on a handle | Moves its edge by a snap step (<kbd>Shift</kbd>: a pixel). |

Editable, the timeline is a `grid`, its tracks `row`s and its regions `gridcell`s named by their `RegionLabel`; handles are `slider`s whose value is the edge's time, announced with `format`.

## Ruler and grid

A grid says where lines fall and what they read. `musicalGrid({ bpm, beatsPerBar, divisions })` gives sixteenths, beats, bars and groups of bars at a constant tempo, read as a song position; `clockGrid()` gives milliseconds to hours, read as `1:05.25`. Both print digits in the user's locale. Make a grid once, outside the component or with `useMemo`.

`Timeline.Ruler` labels the grid; `Timeline.Grid` draws its lines. Each uses the finest step of the grid that leaves `spacing` pixels between lines at the zoom, so zooming out goes from sixteenths to beats, to bars, to groups of bars:

```tsx
<Timeline.Ruler grid={grid} className="h-6 text-xs [&_[data-label]]:ps-1">
  {/* Marks under the labels: a grid in the ruler, from 65% of its height down. */}
  <Timeline.Grid grid={grid} style={{ top: '65%' }} className="text-neutral-600" />
</Timeline.Ruler>

<div className="relative">
  {/* Minor and major lines under the tracks: two grids, the major ones as far apart as the labels. */}
  <Timeline.Grid grid={grid} className="text-white/5" />
  <Timeline.Grid grid={grid} spacing={64} className="text-white/15" />
  {tracks}
</div>
```

Lines are drawn like [waveforms](/docs/components/waveform#how-it-stays-fast), in canvas tiles in the element's CSS `color`, and labels are text placed in time: scrolling and playback draw and render nothing, and the labels change only when the view moves half its width or the zoom changes the step. Labels are `span`s with `data-label`: style them in CSS.

Your own grid is an object with `steps`, seconds between lines from fine to coarse, each a whole multiple of the one before, and `label(time, step)`, for a tempo map or frames of video.

## API reference

### Root

The time axis. Renders a `<div>` with `position: relative`.

<PropsTable part="Timeline.Root" />

### Playhead

A line at the playhead, across everything on the timeline. Renders a `<div>`, hidden from assistive technology: give it a width and a color.

<PropsTable part="Timeline.Playhead" />

### Track

A row of the timeline that holds its regions. Renders a `<div>` with `role="group"` and `position: relative`: name it with `aria-label`.

<PropsTable part="Timeline.Track" />

### Region

Something that lasts on the timeline: a region, clip or pattern, a loop range, a marker. Renders a `<div>` placed and sized in CSS from `at` and `duration`, holding its content: a `group`, or a `gridcell` when editable.

<PropsTable part="Timeline.Region" />

| Data attribute | |
| --- | --- |
| `data-selected` | While the region is selected. |
| `data-dragging` | While a gesture moves or trims it. |

The header, the content and the handles carry them too.

### RegionHeader

The top of a region, for its label and your controls. Renders a `<div>`.

<PropsTable part="Timeline.RegionHeader" />

### RegionLabel

The name of a region, which names it for assistive technology. Renders a `<span>`.

<PropsTable part="Timeline.RegionLabel" />

### RegionContent

Where the content goes: a waveform, notes. Renders a `<div>` with `position: relative`.

<PropsTable part="Timeline.RegionContent" />

### RegionHandle

Trims the start or the end, at that edge, across the region's height. Renders a `<div>` with `role="slider"`, only while the region is active: name it with `aria-label`.

<PropsTable part="Timeline.RegionHandle" />

### Ruler

The labels of a grid: a `<div>` with `position: relative`, holding a `<span data-label>` at every line of the grid `spacing` pixels apart, and its children. Hidden from assistive technology.

<PropsTable part="Timeline.Ruler" />

### Grid

Lines at every step of a grid `spacing` pixels apart: a `<div>` that fills its container, with canvas tiles, in its CSS `color`. Hidden from assistive technology.

<PropsTable part="Timeline.Grid" />
