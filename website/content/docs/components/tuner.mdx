## Anatomy

```tsx
import { formats } from '@addstack/daw-ui';
import { Tuner } from '@addstack/daw-ui/react';

<Tuner.Root read={() => detector.frequency} reference={440} format={formats.pitch({ names: t('noteNames') })} className="group">
  <Tuner.Note />                   {/* "A4" */}
  <Tuner.Frequency />              {/* "440 Hz" */}
  <Tuner.Cents />                  {/* "+12" */}
  <Tuner.Indicator className="left-[calc(50%+var(--tuner-offset)*50%)]" />
  <Tuner.Mark note={69} />
  <Tuner.Strobe />
</Tuner.Root>
```

A tuner takes the frequency your application hears, in hertz, from `read` once per animation frame, or `null` in silence. It detects nothing: pitch detection, from a microphone or an instrument, is your audio code's, as the analyser is for a [spectrum](/docs/components/spectrum). From the frequency and `reference`, the frequency of A4, it reads the note it is nearest and how many cents it is off.

A tuner has no shape. Tuners look like a needle on a scale, an arc, a clock face, a row of lights, a strobe, a wheel of notes; they all show the same few things, and here those are text, attributes and CSS variables your CSS turns into any of them. The demos on this page are three tuners from the same parts.

## What it shows

- `Tuner.Note` is the note, in the root's `format`: `formats.pitch({ names })` for names in your language, or the MIDI note number without one. A note's part can take its own `format`: the demo above shows the note as a name and as a MIDI number.
- `Tuner.Frequency` and `Tuner.Cents` are the frequency and the cents off, with a sign.
- `Tuner.Indicator` is what follows the pitch: a needle, a light, a beam. It sets, on itself only, `--tuner-offset`, the cents off from −50 to +50 as −1 … 1, `--tuner-cents`, and `--tuner-class`, the pitch around an octave from C, 0 to 12 with a fraction, for a wheel.
- `Tuner.Mark` is a mark on the scale, lit with `data-active` while the reading is on it: a note (`note`), a note in any octave (`pitchClass`), or a stretch of cents (`cents={[from, to]}`), such as one light of a row.
- `Tuner.Strobe` sets `--tuner-phase`, the cents off added up over time: a pattern moved by it runs the way the pitch is off, faster the further off, and stands still in tune.

The root, and every part, has `data-active` while a pitch is heard, and `data-in-tune`, `data-flat` or `data-sharp` by `tolerance`, in cents. In silence the texts keep the last reading. Nothing renders as the pitch changes.

## Strings and keys

By default a tuner shows the nearest note. `targets` are the notes a pitch is tuned to instead, as MIDI note numbers: the strings of a guitar, a drop tuning, or the notes of a key, so that a voice snaps to the key as in a voice-to-MIDI tool. `target` tunes to one note, whatever the pitch is nearest, as when a string is chosen by hand:

<ComponentPreview name="tuner-strings" />

```tsx
<Tuner.Root read={() => detector.frequency} targets={[40, 45, 50, 55, 59, 64]} target={chosen}>
  {strings.map((note) => (
    <button key={note} aria-pressed={chosen === note} onClick={() => choose(note)}>
      <Tuner.Mark note={note}>{pitch.format(note)}</Tuner.Mark>
    </button>
  ))}
</Tuner.Root>
```

A pitch between two notes would make the note flicker; the tuner stays on a note until the pitch is `hysteresis` cents nearer another (10). `smoothing`, in milliseconds, makes the reading steadier and slower, as a tuner's reactivity. For a polyphonic tuner, render one tuner per string, each reading its string.

## Accessibility and languages

The root has no role: it can hold your controls, such as the buttons of strings. `Tuner.Note` is a polite live region: a screen reader says the note when it changes, not the cents on every frame. The indicator, the marks and the strobe are hidden from assistive technology. Note names come from your `format`; the numbers follow the locale, and the library adds only "Hz" and signs.
